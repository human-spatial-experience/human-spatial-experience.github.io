01About

Why this workshop

Architectural design shapes human spatial experience: how people perceive, navigate, feel, and act within built environments.

Yet design tools, including increasingly capable AI systems, mostly evaluate geometry and physical performance rather than human experience. Knowledge of spatial experience is spread across cognitive science, neuroscience, environmental psychology, design computing, architectural practice, and HCI, communities that define experience, gather evidence, and inform design in different ways.

This workshop brings these communities together, using HCI as a bridging lens: its focus on people, interaction, and situated decisions can connect experiential evidence with design needs and tool interactions.

Our central question: how can design tools, including AI-aided ones, help designers translate knowledge of spatial experience into design decisions, while keeping its evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations open to inspection?

  1. 1 · Identify where disciplines intersect around concrete design questionsFind design questions that knowledge of spatial experience could help answer and that could be built into tools, such as how a hospital entrance can support wayfinding while preserving privacy, with the applications, evidence, and methods each needs.
  2. 2 · Identify directions for experience-informed tools and workflowsExamine how experiential concerns and evidence could inform briefing, representation, generation, comparison, revision, and learning from buildings in use, and when AI support is or is not appropriate.
  3. 3 · Build a cross-disciplinary research agenda and collaboration pathwaysIdentify frictions that arise when communities work together, such as differing definitions of experience, standards of evidence, or expectations of tools, and turn them into research questions to pursue jointly, building a shared vocabulary along the way.

02Participate

Call for Participation

How can architectural design tools account for how people perceive, navigate, feel, and act in built environments? Knowledge about this experience is spread across cognitive science, neuroscience, environmental psychology, design computing, architecture, AI, and HCI, yet rarely reaches the tools designers use. This workshop brings these communities together to identify concrete design questions where their knowledge meets, explore how experience-informed tools and workflows could support designers and occupants, and build a shared research agenda.

We welcome researchers and practitioners across these fields. We invite contributions that connect findings to design needs, question how AI should participate in design, or pose questions another community could help answer.

  • A spatial-experience concernWhat aspect of how people perceive, navigate, feel, or act in a space does your work address?
  • Evidence or perspectiveResearch, design cases, methods, datasets, or critical perspectives you bring
  • A question for another communityWhat could someone from a different discipline help you answer?
Submissions: position papers (2–4 pages) or short papers (up to 6 pages) presenting a design case, tool or method critique, or research vision, submitted through OpenReview [link TBA]. An implemented AI system is not required. Each submission is reviewed by at least two organizers; we plan to accept about 15 papers.

The workshop expects about 30 participants. All registered CHI attendees may join, with priority for accepted-paper authors; at least one author of each accepted submission is expected to attend in person, and at least two organizers will attend. With authors’ permission, accepted contributions and the workshop synthesis will be shared on this website and an open repository.

Workshop program

Two consecutive 90-minute in-person sessions with one break. Participants work in five interdisciplinary groups of five to seven, each facilitated by an organizer and drawing on at least three disciplines.
